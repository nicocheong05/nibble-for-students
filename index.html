3.2.1 Scaled Dot-Product Attention

We call our version scaled dot-product attention. For each query we take its dot product with every key, divide by √dₖ, and apply a softmax to turn the scores into weights. In practice we compute this for a whole set of queries at once, as in Equation (1).

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKᵀ / √dₖ) V

Dot-product attention is faster and uses less memory than additive attention, the other common choice, because it can run as optimised matrix multiplication.

When dₖ is large, the dot products grow large and push the softmax into regions where its gradients are extremely small. Dividing by √dₖ counteracts this.

4 Why Self-Attention

We compare self-attention with recurrent and convolutional layers on three points. Self-attention links every pair of positions in a constant number of steps, but its cost per layer grows with the square of the sequence length.

1 · The problem

Finding papers has never been easier. Choosing what to read is still on you.

Search engines, citation maps, AI research tools, your supervisor’s reading list, your Zotero folder. A few weeks into a new topic, you’re sitting on dozens of PDFs.

But which papers are central? When you’re new to a field, that call is the hardest to make, because everything looks important.

Get it wrong and you either run out of time on the wrong papers, or hand the whole pile to AI.

2 · The shortcut

So the pile goes to AI, and the reading never happens.

Summaries are fast and feel like progress. A summary gives you the conclusions. It leaves out the reasoning: the figure that doesn’t quite back the claim, the limitation that becomes your own research question.

That reasoning, and the confidence you build by reading, is what makes your proposal, essay or thesis your work. You only get it by reading the paper.

A summary isn’t the paper.

Up to 73% of AI summaries overstated what the paper found.

Peters & Chin-Yee, 2025

Strong readers understood less from a summary than from the text itself.

Etkin et al., 2025
3 · What Nibble does

Nibble does the choosing. You do the reading.

Researchers spend almost as much time finding papers as they do reading them.

Nibble takes on the hesitating, the worrying, the sorting and the organising, so that time goes back into reading. It also remembers the path you took and the thinking behind it, so you can always see how you got here.

FAQ

Questions students ask us

How is this different from asking ChatGPT what to read?

ChatGPT can give you a good reading plan on day one. Research isn't a one-off question: your question changes, your supervisor adds papers, and you forget which ones you've read or skipped. With a chatbot, you have to keep track of all that and explain it again every time.

Nibble keeps it for you. It remembers what you've read and set aside, keeps your supervisor's must-reads in the plan, and when your question changes, it re-plans from where you are instead of starting over. Every decision comes with a reason you can check, so you can always see how you got here.

Does it replace Zotero or EndNote?

No. Import from them and keep your library where it is.

Which subjects does it work for?

Any subject where you read papers. You tell Nibble your field and your background, and screening takes both into account.
